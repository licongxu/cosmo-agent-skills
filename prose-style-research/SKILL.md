# Research prose style

## No em dashes

Do **not** use em dashes (U+2014) in manuscript text, captions, or user-facing reports.

Use instead:

- **Comma or parentheses** for aside clauses.
- **Colon** to introduce a list or explanation.
- **Separate sentences** when the break is strong.
- **En dash only** for numeric ranges (e.g. 10--20 samples, 0.5--2.0 s).

Hyphenate compound modifiers (`cross-validated`, `end-to-end`).

## Clarity

- One idea per sentence where possible; avoid long chains of subordinate clauses.
- State what failed and why; do not hide limitations only in the final section.
- Prefer concrete identifiers (`method v2`, `N=512`) over vague "the model".
- Before heavy notation, one plain sentence: what the quantity is and why it matters.

## Captions

- Captions are prose: full sentences, units, sample size, method or version id.
- No em dashes; avoid hype ("remarkably", "clearly superior") without supporting numbers.

## Consistency with figures

- Text and captions must match the **current** figure files and run identifiers.
- Numbers in the abstract and conclusion must trace to the same artifacts as the figures.

## Self-check before submitting text

Search the draft for `—` and replace every instance.
